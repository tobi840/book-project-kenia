Subject: Eastern Bongo (Tragelaphus eurycerus isaaci). The Eastern Bongo is a massive, stocky forest antelope characterized by a deep russet-red coat that darkens significantly, sometimes appearing almost black, in mature males. Across its flanks, the animal displays between eight and fifteen thin, asymmetrical white-to-yellow vertical stripes that serve as disruptive camouflage in the dappled forest light. The head features remarkably large, broad ears cupped forward, a distinct white chevron across the bridge of the muzzle, and thick, ivory-tipped horns that spiral upward in a single, tight twist. When navigating dense undergrowth, the bongo tilts its head sharply backward, laying its heavy horns flat along the spine to prevent entanglement in low-hanging lianas.
