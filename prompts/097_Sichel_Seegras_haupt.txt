Subject: Sichel-Seegras, Thalassodendron ciliatum. Thalassodendron ciliatum displays a highly unusual architecture for a marine plant, resembling a miniature underwater palm tree rather than a typical grass. A stiff, woody, and unbranched stem rises vertically from a thick, creeping subterranean rhizome, culminating at its peak in a dense, fan-like crown of curved, ribbon-like leaves. The linear, sickle-shaped leaves measure roughly 10 to 15 centimeters in length and 10 millimeters in width, exhibiting a vibrant green hue that is frequently tinged or longitudinally striped with a deep, earthy red. In coastal currents, the entire rigid stem bows slightly while the fan-shaped crown flutters horizontally, anchored into the coarse sand solely by robust, highly branched root clusters that grip the substrate at strict intervals along the underlying rhizome.
