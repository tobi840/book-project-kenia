Subject: Kathstrauch, Catha edulis. Catha edulis is a densely branching, evergreen shrub exhibiting a rigid, upright growth habit, characterized by straight, cylindrical stems that feature smooth, reddish-pink bark on young peripheral shoots, which gradually thickens into a finely fissured, grayish-brown exterior on the main trunk. The foliage is strictly opposite, consisting of coriaceous, lance-shaped leaves measuring 4 to 12 centimeters in length, presenting a highly glossy, dark emerald-green upper lamina that sharply contrasts with a matte, paler green underside, all framed by finely serrated edges that curl slightly downward. Clustered tightly in the leaf axils are short, compact inflorescences bearing multiple microscopic flowers, each spanning only 5 to 8 millimeters across, equipped with five fleshy, pale-white to yellowish petals and matching delicate white stamens. The fruiting bodies are distinctively small, oblong, three-valved capsules measuring up to 10 millimeters, ripening from light green to a deeply rich reddish-brown hue.
