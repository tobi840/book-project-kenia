Subject: Von der Decken's hornbill (Tockus deckeni). The Von der Decken's hornbill is a slender, medium-small bird displaying starkly contrasting plumage: its underparts, neck, and head are pure white, while its back, central tail feathers, and unspotted wing coverts are a deep blackish-brown. Its most defining physical trait is the dramatically down-curved, laterally flattened bill which lacks any prominent casque on the upper mandible. In adult males, this oversized bill is split into two bold colors—the posterior half is vivid carmine red, while the anterior half and tip are a pale creamy-yellow, sharply defined by a black cutting edge. The bird is typically seen adopting a somewhat upright, alert posture on the ground, scanning the dry earth with its dark brown eyes surrounded by a bare ring of black orbital skin.
