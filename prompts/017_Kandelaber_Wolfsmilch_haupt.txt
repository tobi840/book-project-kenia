Subject: Kandelaber-Wolfsmilch, Euphorbia candelabrum. The Euphorbia candelabrum stands as a monumental, succulent tree, anchored by a robust, grey, roughly fissured bole that can reach nearly a meter in diameter before rapidly diverging. From about three meters upward, this central trunk splinters into a dense, broadly rounded crown resembling an immense, geometric candelabra. The fleshy, vertical branches are strictly upright, lacking any foliage, and are strongly four-angled with deep constrictions occurring every 15 to 25 centimeters, creating an articulated, sausage-like appearance. The surfaces are a pale green-grey, lined precisely along the outer ridges with shallow teeth and rigid thorns, affording the plant a rigid, architectural severity rather than fluid botanical curves.
