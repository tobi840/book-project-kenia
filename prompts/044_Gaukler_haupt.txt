Subject: Bateleur eagle (Terathopius ecaudatus). The Bateleur eagle exhibits a highly distinct, somewhat top-heavy silhouette characterized by an exceptionally large, thickly cowled head and a disproportionately short, almost non-existent tail. When perched, it maintains a remarkably upright, regal posture on relatively short, heavily scaled, bright red legs, giving the illusion of a taller raptor. The adult male’s plumage presents a stark, bold contrast: a jet-black body and head, pale grey wing shoulders that appear slightly pinched at their bases, and a rich, deep chestnut coloration across the mantle and back. In flight, the wings are exceptionally long, narrow, and slightly bow-shaped, revealing white underwing coverts bordered by a thick black trailing edge across its 25 secondary feathers. The most arresting focal point is the face, dominated by a large, brilliantly red, unfeathered cere that sharply transitions into a short, yellow-orange beak with a pronounced black hook.
