Subject: Hunter-Antilope / Hirola (Beatragus hunteri). The Hirola (Beatragus hunteri) is a medium-sized, gracefully built antelope with a uniformly tawny to rufous-brown coat that pales to a creamy white on the belly and the inner sides of the limbs. Its elongated face is marked by a striking, inverted white chevron forming a distinct "V" between the eyes, seamlessly connecting two sharp white rings that encircle the eyes like spectacles. The thick, heavily annulated black horns rise vertically from the skull without a basal pedicle, curving sharply outwards before hooking steeply upwards and backwards in a distinct, elongated lyre shape. The overall posture reveals a horizontal back with withers only slightly elevated above the hindquarters, terminating in a long tail tipped with a prominent tuft of mixed black and white hairs. Below the spectacle markings sit exceptionally large, dark preorbital glands that visually dominate the pale facial fur, earning the species its moniker as the four-eyed antelope.
