Subject: Kirk's dik-dik (Madoqua kirkii). The Kirk's dik-dik is an exceptionally diminutive antelope, standing no higher than an adult human's knee, with a distinctly wedge-shaped head that tapers into a highly mobile, elongated snout overhanging the lower jaw. Its hind legs are visibly larger and more muscular than the slender, pipestem-like forelegs, giving the animal a slightly hunched, hare-like posture poised for sudden movement. The coat is a finely grizzled mix of yellowish-gray and reddish-brown on the back, transitioning to chestnut on the flanks and fading to white on the underbelly. The face is marked by striking white rings completely encircling the large, dark eyes, and a prominent, hairless, dark preorbital gland slit situated just below the inside corner of each eye. In males, short, backwards-slanted, deeply corrugated horns rise from the forehead, though they are frequently obscured by a prominent tuft of erectile rufous hair that forms a crest between the ears.
