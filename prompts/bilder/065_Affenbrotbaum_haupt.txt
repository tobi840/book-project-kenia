Subject: Affenbrotbaum (African baobab), Adansonia digitata. The African baobab stands as a colossal, pachycaul monolith, its trunk forming a swollen, irregular cylinder of purplish-grey, heavily scarred bark that flares abruptly at the base into robust, buttressed roots. During the harsh dry season, the canopy is entirely barren, thrusting thick, gnarled, and rapidly tapering branches into the sky like an upended root system. When the tree flushes, it bears palmately compound foliage, and from long, drooping peduncles hang massive, pendulous white flowers with deeply reflexed, crumpled petals that expose a prominent, spherical brush of hundreds of delicate white stamens. Accent colour: bright yellow-green, used sparingly in the capsule fuzz and exposed photosynthetic tissue.
